We establish sharp information bounds and a finite certified method for computing linear functionals of positive relaxation measures from frequency data. For the resistance–capacitance kernel on a compact interval of positive relaxation times, with unknown nonnegative resistance and inductance, exactly K+2 distinct frequencies are required for uniform noiseless identification of references with at most K atoms among arbitrary finite positive competitors. This minimal design also attains the same worst-class functional accuracy order as the entire positive-frequency response. The sharp exponent is determined by target regularity and cluster multiplicity, uniformly through colliding atoms and vanishing masses. Positive moment-matched alternatives supply all-frequency lower bounds, while nuisance-annihilating polynomial reproduction supplies matching upper bounds. On strictly feasible rational observation boxes, we compute both endpoints of the original data-consistent range by verified finite optimization and continuous polynomial inequalities. A finite-budget refinement algorithm terminates at any prescribed numerical excess, and computable width brackets give absolute–relative stopping criteria. The same functional modulus determines the optimal expected acquisition effort for prescribed terminal width and confidence in a Gaussian variance–effort experiment, including adaptive frequency, precision, and stopping choices. A class-tuned expanding design attains this order and completes pointwise over the full positive-measure model. Exact certificates and stopping-path computations connect the information bounds to executable range calculations.
